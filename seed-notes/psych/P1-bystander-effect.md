# The bystander effect

People are less likely to help someone in trouble when other people are around.
The more bystanders there are, the smaller the chance that any single person steps
in to help.

The main reason is diffusion of responsibility: when many people witness the same
situation, each person feels that helping is not specifically their job, and assumes
someone else will act. As a result, everyone waits, and often no one helps at all.

A simple way to overcome it is to make responsibility personal. If you are the one
in need, point at a specific person and ask them directly, so the duty to act falls
on a single named individual instead of the crowd.
