# When crowds freeze: why nobody steps forward

A familiar pattern in emergencies is that a person surrounded by onlookers gets less
help than a person who is alone with one passer-by. Adding more witnesses tends to
lower, not raise, the odds that someone intervenes.

Beyond the sense that the duty is shared and therefore nobody's, a second force is
at work: people read each other's calm faces and conclude nothing is really wrong.
Because everyone is privately uncertain and outwardly composed, the group talks
itself into inaction. This mutual misreading is called pluralistic ignorance.

Naming a single helper still breaks the spell — once one person is singled out and
acts, others usually follow.
